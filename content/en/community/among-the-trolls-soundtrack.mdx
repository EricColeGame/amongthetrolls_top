Released alongside Avantgarden SRL's first-person narrative adventure on October 14, 2026, the score pairs composed music with recorded Nordic ambience to make the Finnish wilderness feel inhabited rather than merely rendered. If you are deciding whether to add the **Among the Trolls soundtrack** to your library, or you simply want to understand why so many early players mention the audio in their reviews, this guide covers what is confirmed, what is still unclear, and how the music functions inside the game itself.

The store description explicitly frames the whole experience as an "audiovisual" one, where the visuals are paired with an enchanting score and the authentic sounds of Nordic nature. That is not marketing filler — for a game about lost family history and Finnish mythology, sound is doing a large share of the storytelling.

## How the Music Shapes the Finnish Mythology Setting

Among the Trolls drops you into Anna's shoes as she arrives at her grandfather's isolated cabin and finds it abandoned. From there, the game leans on Finnish mythology, shamanistic magic, and the living, animistic forces of nature. The audio design carries a huge amount of that weight, because so much of the tension comes from things you cannot see.

Two systems in particular are tied to the atmosphere the score creates: Väki, the game's take on magic, and Lykky, its luck mechanic. Players are asked to balance the two while surviving harsh weather, wild animals, and scarce resources. Audio cues are one of the main ways the game communicates when those forces are shifting.

| Audio layer | Role in the game | Why it matters to players |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composed score | Underscores story beats and mythology reveals | Signals when a moment is narratively important |
| Ambient nature beds | Wind, water, birds, insects, creaking wood | Makes the forest feel continuous rather than staged |
| Creature and supernatural cues | Hints at approaching threats and otherworldly presence | Provides warning when visibility is poor |
| Silence and negative space | Used before encounters and during exploration | Makes the loud moments land harder |

The practical effect is that the Among the Trolls soundtrack is not just a playlist you might enjoy separately. Inside the game, it is a navigation and tension tool. Turning the music down to zero to "focus" often makes the wilderness harder to read, not easier.

### Why Nordic nature sounds are doing double duty

The game's own description points to "the authentic sounds of Nordic nature" as a core part of the audiovisual package. That phrasing matters. A lot of forest-set games layer a generic wind loop over everything and call it ambience. Here, the natural soundscape is presented as a feature in its own right, sitting alongside the composed music rather than underneath it.

For players interested in the Kalevala-inspired creatures and the mythology running through the story, the soundscape is often the first hint that something from that world has entered the scene. Pay attention to it.
