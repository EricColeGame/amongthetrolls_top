## Story and Setting: Finnish Mythology Takes Center Stage

The narrative hook is simple and effective. You play as Anna, a woman who travels from the United States to Finland to visit her grandfather at a remote cabin deep in the woods. When she arrives, the cabin is empty. Her grandfather has vanished, and the search pulls her into a world where Nordic mythology is not a story people tell but a force that acts on them.

The trailer communicates this transition visually. What begins as a family drama gradually gives way to something stranger, with imagery rooted in shamanistic tradition and creatures drawn from the classic Kalevala myths. The phrase the developers use is "the living, animistic forces of Nature" — and the footage supports that framing.

If you have played narrative adventures built around a single strong location, the structure will feel familiar. What sets this one apart is the specificity of its folklore. Finnish mythology is far less represented in games than Norse mythology, and the trailer makes that distinction clear.

## Gameplay Systems the Trailer Confirms

The trailer is a launch video, not a tutorial, so it shows systems rather than explaining them. Still, the store page fills in the gaps, and together they paint a clear picture of the loop you will be repeating.

### Survival in an Unforgiving Wilderness

Weather, wild animals, and scarce resources all push back against you. The environment is described as breathtaking but unforgiving, which is a polite way of saying that you will spend a meaningful amount of time managing your own survival before you can chase the plot.

### Väki and Lykky: The Two-Resource Twist

This is the most distinctive mechanical idea in the game. Väki is a Finnish concept of magical power, and Lykky is luck. Rather than treating them as flavor text, the game asks you to balance both, and it implies that the two forces are intertwined. Watching the trailer, you can see moments where the environment reacts to something the player has done — a strong hint that these systems are always running in the background.

### Building, Crafting, and Archery

The player-defined tags on the store page read like a checklist of the trailer's second half: crafting, archery, exploration, mystery, and 3D first-person survival. You build, you adapt, and you unlock ancestral secrets as you go.

A few practical takeaways for anyone still on the fence:

- **Treat the sample size with caution.** Twenty-six reviews is a snapshot, not a verdict. Narrative games in particular often see scores shift as players finish the story.
- **Watch the trailer for tone, not for scope.** The Among the Trolls official launch trailer sells atmosphere and premise effectively, but it does not promise a hundred-hour open world.
- **Check the demo feedback.** The developers explicitly said they gathered and acted on demo feedback before launch, which suggests the launch build differs from what early testers played.
- **Consider your tolerance for survival systems.** If resource management and crafting feel like chores to you, the survival layer here is substantial enough to matter.

Player experience varies most around pacing. Some early reviewers describe the opening hours as slow and atmospheric; others wanted more direction. That split is visible in the mixed rating and is worth factoring into your expectations.
