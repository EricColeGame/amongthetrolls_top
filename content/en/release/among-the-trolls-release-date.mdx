## What the Game Actually Is

Strip away the marketing language and the premise is straightforward. You play as Anna, who travels from the United States to a remote Finnish cabin to visit her grandfather — and arrives to find the cabin abandoned and him missing. That disappearance pulls her into a world where Finnish mythology is not decorative backdrop but an active, hostile presence.

The developers describe the experience as a first-person narrative adventure with survival elements. That's accurate, but the more interesting detail is how the survival layer is framed: rather than a generic hunger-and-thirst loop, it's built around Väki (magic) and Lykky (luck), two concepts pulled from Finnish folk tradition. Balancing them is presented as a core mechanic rather than a stat screen afterthought.

| Design Pillar | How It Shows Up in Play |
| --- | --- |
| Find your roots | A mystery-driven quest to locate Anna's missing grandfather |
| Natural and supernatural threats | Harsh weather, wildlife, and scarce resources alongside mythic encounters |
| Väki and Lykky | Magic and luck systems that must be balanced to survive |
| Finnish mythology | Creatures and concepts drawn from the Kalevala tradition |
| Survive the wild | Building, crafting, and adapting to an unforgiving environment |
| Audiovisual presentation | A stylized 3D world paired with authentic Nordic nature sound |

Community reports and store tags reinforce this framing — "Story Rich," "Mythology," "Atmospheric," and "Survival" all appear prominently. Notably, "Female Protagonist" and "Lore-Rich" are among the most-applied tags, which suggests players are engaging with the narrative side rather than treating it as a pure crafting sandbox.
